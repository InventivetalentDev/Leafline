# Leafline: technical design

What to build and why it is shaped this way. Decisions are firm unless marked **Verify** (an engine behaviour to confirm in UE 5.8 before relying on it) or **Tune** (a number to adjust by play). The game rules are in `design.md`.

## The central idea: one distance field

The tree is defined by a skeleton of branches. From that skeleton we derive a **signed distance function** `D(p)`: the distance from any point to the tree's surface, negative inside. Everything that touches the tree asks `D` instead of using physics collision:

| Consumer | What it asks |
|---|---|
| Ant movement | Project my position back onto the surface; which way is "up" here |
| Camera | March from the ant to my desired position; stop before the tree |
| Wind | Is this point facing the wind, and is the tree upwind of it |
| Trail and replay | Re-project recorded points onto the surface |
| Leaf and nest placement | Where exactly is the surface |

Why this shape:
- Walking on collision meshes gives jittery normals and gets pawns wedged where a branch meets its parent. A smooth field has a continuous gradient everywhere, including at junctions, so both problems cannot occur.
- No collision cooking for a tree that is regenerated at runtime.
- Unattended ants need no navigation: they interpolate a recorded path and project onto the field.

`D` is cheap enough to brute-force for the handful of queries per frame we need (see Performance).

## Units and scale

Unreal units are centimetres. One **ant length (AL) = 20 cm**. This is about 25 times life size, which keeps camera near-planes and float precision comfortable.

| Quantity | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Ant body length | 20 cm | 1 AL |
| Trunk radius at chest height | 750 cm | circumference about 47 m, 235 AL |
| Tree height | about 300 m | a 12 m tree at 25x |
| Walk / sprint / trail-ride speed | 200 / 400 / 600 cm/s | **Tune**. Walk circles the trunk in about 24 s |
| Leaf length | about 200 cm | 10 AL |
| Fragment diameter | 30–100 cm | 1.5–5 AL |
| Lowest leaves, day 1 | 30 to 40 m up | first cut inside 90 s. Leafy from 20 m on the first tree (26 and 32 m on the next, `FDayTuning::Stages`), but the sparse low zone thins the first 45 to 55 m, so the first leaves sit at 30 to 40 m on all three trees (`Tools/tree_bands_measured.txt`) |

## Module layout

One runtime module, `AntTreeGame`. Folders under `Source/AntTreeGame/`:

```
Tree/      TreeTypes.h, TreeSkeleton, TreeField, TreeMeshBuilder, TreeSkirts.inl, TreeActor, LeafSet
Ant/       AntPawn (also the ant's look), AntMovementComponent (surface walker), AntCamera
Colony/    ColonyTypes.h (tuning structs), LineSystem, TrailNetwork, NestActor
World/     WindSystem, WindCues, DayDirector (also the worker roster), BirdSystem, ExposureBands.h, ExposureOverlay, ForestFloor, GroundRelief
Game/      LeaflineGameMode, LeaflineGameInstance, LeaflineSaveGame, LeaflinePlayerController, Upgrades, LeaflineShots
Audio/     LeaflineAudioSubsystem, LeafSound
UI/        LeaflineHUD (HUD and menu screens), PromptIcons, Skin, Credits
Debug/     console commands (each folder may also hold its own *Commands.cpp)
```

All gameplay is C++. Blueprint subclasses exist only to hold tuning values and asset references. Anything an agent has to edit should be text: input actions, mapping contexts and UI are created in C++ rather than as editor assets. Materials are the unavoidable binary assets and are built through the editor MCP.

## Tree

### Skeleton (`TreeSkeleton`)

Pure data, no engine objects, deterministic from a seed (`FRandomStream`).

```
FTreeNode   { FVector Pos; float Radius; }
FTreeBranch { TArray<FTreeNode> Nodes; int32 Parent; float ParentT; int32 Level; FBox Bounds; }
FTreeSkeleton { TArray<FTreeBranch> Branches; FTreeParams Params; int32 Seed; }
```

- Level 0 is the trunk, 1 limbs, 2 boughs, 3 twigs. Leaves grow on levels 2 and 3.
- The trunk has a slight lean and curve and a **root flare** (radius grows toward the ground). Add three to five short root branches that dive into the soil; the nest sits between two of them.
- Children leave the parent at a golden-angle spacing around it with jitter, pitch upward, and take a fraction of the parent's radius at that point.
- Growth avoids existing branches: test each new segment against grown branches, bend away inside a margin, and stop the branch if it would still pass through one.
- `FTreeParams` holds every knob, including `LowestLeafyHeight`, which `DayDirector` sets: from the stage's `FStageTuning` with one tree per stage, else rising each day.
- A branch centreline is evaluated as a Catmull-Rom curve through its nodes so the surface has no facets.

### Field (`TreeField`)

```
float   Distance(const FVector& P) const;            // D(p)
FVector Gradient(const FVector& P) const;            // normalised, outward
bool    Project(FVector& P, FVector& OutNormal, float Height) const;
bool    March(const FVector& From, const FVector& To, float Radius, float& OutT) const;
```

- Per branch: the pieces between consecutive mesh rings. Each piece is bounded by the two ring planes exactly as `TreeMeshBuilder` orients its rings (central-difference tangents), and blends centre, radius and axis from one ring to the next by where the point sits between the planes, so the field passes through every ring and neighbours agree on their shared plane: no gap on the outer side of a bend, no overlap on the inner side. Only the branch's two ends are rounded caps. (Earlier versions capped every joint with a sphere, which bulged metres out of the root flare, then used cones bounded by planes perpendicular to their own axes, which left a ridge up to 20 cm tall on the outer side of every slight bend: the day-4 "can't climb past this line" bug.) `Leaf.MeshGap` checks the field against every visible mesh vertex.
- The ground plane carries the nest mound, a half-buried ellipsoid sized by `FTreeParams::NestMoundRadius`/`NestMoundHeight`; `ANestActor::SetMoundSize` scales the visible mound to match. Since look pass 2 the visible mound is `SM_Nest`, generated by `Tools/blender/make_nest.py` for 120 × 7 cm (the field's surface solved per radius, with its 4 cm blend into the ground; exact only at that size), loaded by path with the sphere and disc as fallback. The entrance is vertex colour and inward-leaning normals over the walking surface, not a hole, because ants stand on it; pellets and piles are at most 8 mm high, leaf scraps 4.5 mm. Slot 0 `M_NestSoil` (`Tools/materials/nest_soil.hlsl`; vertex colour R soil cover, G entrance, B pellet mask, A spoil or per-clump random, see `Art/Blender/README.md`), slot 1 `M_Leaf`. No deposit pulse on it: scaling lifts the rim off the walking surface.
- **Forest floor** (`AForestFloor`, `World/ForestFloor.*`, 14 Nov): visual litter beyond a bare clearing, spawned by the game mode, built once per tree seed (`FRandomStream(seed * 104729 + 9161)`) when the tree has grown, in 8 angular sectors of procedural meshes (6 colour sections each, `M_Litter` instances with `BasicShapeMaterial` as fallback, no shadows, no collision). Not `LeafSet`, so saves and leaf counts are unaffected. The bare clearing is a disc round the trunk (flared radius + 3 m), a disc round each nest spot (4 m), a corridor from each nest spot to the trunk (3.5 m either side) and a band of 1.5 m beyond every root's own radius, with a wavy edge (`FForestFloorTuning`). Outside it: flat leaves lie 1.5 to 2.7 mm above the ground (the trail ribbons are at 1.2 cm) for 1.5 m; beyond that curled and tilted leaves, twigs and grass tufts (3 to 5 blades, dark olive-green, 2 to 4 m tall at full height, about half that at the edge; foot at least 2.5 m past the flat ring, so 6 to 7 m from a nest) start. Grass clumps thin out as e^(-d/22 m) with the distance d from the bare ground, so every side of the clearing has grass at its edge. Litter and grass are stratified by angle (24 sectors, an equal share each, clumps inside), so no side of the trunk is favoured; 12% of the clumps reach the litter radius (the rest lie within `GrassRadius`, 70 m, of the trunk axis), and taller far tufts out to 300 m break the horizon. The log prints the near tufts per compass octant and by distance. Leaves are brown, dull tan-ochre, olive and dark brown; none orange. About 3,400 leaves, 2,850 tufts (11,400 blades), 90 twigs, 415,000 triangles, built in 50 ms. Why the tufts once did not read: at an ant's eye height everything beyond 10 m flattens into the horizon, so grass 30 m and farther out (and 1 m tall) looked like stubble; the grass now starts 6 m from a nest and is 2 to 4 m tall (an ant is 20 cm, so about 10 to 20 ant lengths). `Leaf.FloorView` logs how many tufts lie in a 60 degree cone in front of the ant by distance. The grass blades use `M_Grass` (`Tools/materials/grass.hlsl`, `build_grass_mcp.py`; falls back to `M_Litter`): each blade carries UV0 = (height fraction, random phase) and UV1.x = its height in metres, and the material bends it downwind by world position offset from `MPC_Wind` (`WindStrength`, `WindDirection`) and Time, a steady lean plus an out-of-step flutter, both growing with the wind; the meshes carry `BoundsScale` 1.6 so swaying blades are not culled. Every vertex is checked before the meshes are built (`Sanitize`): a triangle with a non-finite position, normal or UV is dropped and the log says so (`Forest floor: no non-finite vertices` is the healthy line). Until 14 Nov evening every leaf tip was NaN (`pow(sin(pi) = -8.7e-8, 0.9)`), which gave some sector components NaN bounds, so the renderer's `NaNs found on Bounds` ensure fired and the component was culled whole (no grass or litter on that side). `Leaf.Floor 0|1` hides it, `Leaf.TopDown` shoots it from above.
- **The floor seen from the tree** (floor pass, 14 Nov evening, `docs/briefs/floor-from-above.md`):
  - **Haze.** The height fog and the volumetric fog start 15 m from the camera (`FogStartDistance`, `VolumetricFogStartDistance` 1500, `VolumetricFogNearFadeIn` 600); `FogSkyTint` scales the sky's colour in the fog (left at white). The start leaves the nest view and the light-shaft views (trunk, canopy, sunward on seed 517192) unchanged within 1 to 5 grey levels. Density stays 0.06. In a clean test (wind held calm, no streaks or cues: `Leaf.Tune wind CalmMin 400`, `CalmMax 400`, `StreakCount 0`, `Leaf.Cues 0`) five times the density (0.3) washed out the floor seen from 60 m up (median 147 to 177, its spread 137..163 to 170..183) and nearly erased the tree's shadow on it, and hazed the nest's horizon (157 to 187), so heavy fog is out; values between were not tested cleanly. (Earlier tests showed a white cloud by the trunk that looked like fog: it was the gust's dust-front cue, which arrives every 15 s or so, so screenshot runs for the look should hold the wind calm.) The far look of the ground material is what lowers the floor's contrast and brightness.
  - **M_Ground2** (`Tools/materials/ground2.hlsl`, `build_ground2_mcp.py`): a duplicate of `M_Ground` with one Custom node, `GroundFar`, between M_Ground's own colour, roughness and normal and the outputs, so the near look is M_Ground's exactly. From `FarStart` (18 m from the camera) to `FarEnd` (50 m) it blends to a carpet of leaf-shaped flecks on a 1.1 m grid (`Fleck`), each a tone of the litter palette, dark soil between, faint variation at 6 and 25 m, darker and greyer than M_Ground (mean about half its brightness), fading to that mean where a pixel covers a quarter of a fleck; and a shading-only undulation (`UndSize` 4.5 m; `Undulate` 0.3 in the material, 0.15 set on the instance by `PickGround`, since 0.3 read as a dimpled mud flat from 120 m up). The game loads it by path onto a dynamic instance (`ATreeActor::PickGround`), with M_Ground as the fallback; `Leaf.Ground 0|1 [Name Value ...]` switches and tunes it. The nest's `M_NestSoil` still matches the near M_Ground tone. Measured (seed 675856, owner's pose `Leaf.Goto 504 1008 5966 -37 -14`): the floor's median grey went from 165 to 145 against 153 to 157 for sunlit bark, so the floor now sits below sunlit bark; auto exposure lifts the frame when the floor darkens, so `FarDark` changes little on screen. Straight down (`Leaf.TopDown` 10 to 80 m) the change shows only as a soft radial gradient at about 30 m up, no line; the nest views are unchanged. GPU time +0.1 ms (1280 x 720, with and without ray tracing).
  - **Relief beyond the ants' reach** (`World/GroundRelief.*`, `LeafRelief`; reworked 14 Nov night, **on by default** since the owner's decision of 15 Nov, `Leaf.Relief 0` gives the flat plane). One height function, zero inside 90 m of the trunk axis or the crown's reach plus 22 m, whichever is farther (a blown-off ant drifts at most 16.6 m from where it leaves the bark in still air: at most 5.8 m/s sideways, drag 0.35/s; glancing off branches can turn falling speed sideways, so that is not a hard bound; 4,027 logged landings reach at most 80.0 m, p99.9 73.6 m; the walking boundary turns ants back from 70 m, fully at 78 m; on seeds 675856, 517192 and 15161 the crowns reach less than 68 m, so the start is 90 m), a smooth ramp to 220 m, then value-noise bands: near ones of 80 and 240 m (half-heights 2.5 and 7 m) and far ones of 900 m, 3 km and 6 km (25, 50 and 60 m, times the far factor 4; they grow in with distance), each faded out where the rings get too coarse for it (four rings per wavelength). A rim lifts the ground from 1.5 km to the 5 km mark by 250 m (55 to 100% along a 2 km band) and the rings reach 30 km while it is on, so from the tree the outline is a ridge line instead of the ground's ruled edge: the "ruled line" was the far edge of the 5 km disk, fogged to a darker blue than the sky. The ground rings are 4% apart beyond 80 m and take their normals and tangents from the function; the grove trunks stand on it, sunk by the drop across their foot (the log line `Grove on the relief` gives the lowest margin between a trunk's bottom and the ground round its foot: at least 2.9 m on the three seeds); the litter, twigs and every grass tuft sit on it (top-down over the 100 to 140 m ring: no leaf lost in a hollow). The walking field stays a plane. Measured (calm wind, `Tools/floorfar_plans/relief_final.txt`): from 94, 121 and 188 m up the horizon becomes rolling hills and a ridge line (mean change 5 to 7 grey levels), also on seeds 517192 and 15161 and in the video's reveal shot; from the treetop the dark band turns into a lighter, softer one but stays straight at the left; the view down from the owner's pose barely changes (the brief's slopes that catch light in the view down are not achieved: the floor 100 to 250 m out lies in haze); from the nest faint grey hills show behind the grass; from open ground at the walking boundary the rim reads as a pale flat-topped band (`Leaf.Relief 1 90 1 4 0 30` leaves the rim out). Near bands at three times their height still did not read from the tree (the floor 100 to 250 m out lies in haze), so they stay as they are. Frame time (1280 x 720, ray tracing, off/on/off/on): GPU 3.4 ms off and on at the owner's pose, 3.7 against 3.8 ms at the canopy horizon; render thread 4.9 ms off and on in a quiet run. `Leaf.Relief [0|1] [start m] [near] [far] [rim m] [ground km]` rebuilds the ground, the grove and the floor; the first version (from 160 m, far x1, no rim, 5 km) did not show at all.
- **The ant's look**: `M_Ant2` (a duplicate of `M_Ant` with the colour side replaced by one Custom node, `Tools/materials/ant_look.hlsl`, built by `Tools/materials/build_ant2_mcp.py`) takes `HeadThoraxColor`, `GasterColor`, `LegColor`, `Roughness`, `Fill` and `RimStrength` from `AAntPawn::LookA` / `LookB` (`FAntLookTuning`). The gaster is masked by mesh X (behind -5.2 cm). The gait chain is untouched. `Leaf.AntLook 0|1|2`; without `M_Ant2` the old look stays.
- Combine all branches **and the ground** with a polynomial smooth minimum. The blend radius `k` gives a small fillet at every junction and at the root flare. Start with `k` = 30% of the child's radius, capped at 1.5 AL. **Tune.**
- `Gradient` by central differences is fine (six extra evaluations).
- `Project` moves a point to the level set `D = Height` with three Newton steps: `P -= Gradient(P) * (D(P) - Height)`.
- `March` is sphere tracing, used by the camera and the wind.
- Acceleration: an AABB per branch, skipped when the query point is farther than the current best distance. Add a uniform grid of segment indices only if profiling asks for it.

- **Fillets** (`FTreeParams::bFillets`, default on; `Leaf.Fillets 0|1 [frac] [max] [boughmul] [groundk] [twigmin]` regrows today's tree). With `bFilletWalk` (default on since 14 Nov evening; `Leaf.FilletWalk 0|1` regrows today's tree) the walking field (`ATreeActor::GetField`, which everything in the sim asks: walking, recruits, trails, falls, wind, exposure) has them, so the ants walk over the rounded junctions the mesh shows; the skirts lie on the same field (`GetVisualField` returns it). With `bFilletWalk` off they are looks only: the walking field has none, a second field with them (`SkirtField`) shapes the skirts, and what is drawn is lifted onto them (below). In the field with fillets, where a limb, bough or root leaves its parent, the branch blends in with a fillet radius `FilletK` instead of `BlendK`:
  - `FilletK` = `FilletFrac` (0.7) × the child's base radius, capped at `FilletMax` (150 cm); boughs are multiplied by `FilletBoughMul` (1).
  - The fillet radius applies only inside the junction zone (`FTreeBranch::FilletCenter/Inner/Outer`), set after growth by `FinishFillets` (no random numbers). It is full within R / sin(a) + 2K of the point where the child's axis leaves the parent (a is the angle between the axis and the parent's surface, at least 0.25), and eases back to `BlendK` over another K.
  - A branch folded into the field before the child (trunk, roots, earlier limb groups, earlier siblings) that lies within R / sin(a) + 1.5K of the centre would be webbed to the child. In that case the fillet shrinks to fit, or is dropped when it would be no bigger than the plain blend (logged; 0 to 1 per tree on the three test seeds).
  - The broken stub gets no fillet.
  - Outside every zone the field is exactly the field without fillets: same blend, same cull margin. Inside a zone the cull margin is 3 × that zone's K.
  - Twigs get fillets too (owner, 14 Nov: "small branches should have fillets too") when their base radius is at least `FilletTwigMinRadius` (3 cm; on the three stage trees every twig is 6 cm or more, so all of them: 827, 643 and 1052). Their skirts sweep at 6° instead of 3° (fillet radius under 10 cm). Cost on trees 1/2/3: 954/745/1225 skirts (810/632/1041 twigs), 203k/175k/231k skirt triangles (107k/99k/111k without twigs), 105–210 ms per skirt pass. 1 to 2% of twig skirts fail (ring or end) and lose their fillet, and the mesh is then laid a second time, so a tree grows in about 0.9–1.15 s instead of 0.6–0.75 s. `Leaf.Fillets 1 -1 -1 -1 -1 1000` turns twig fillets off.
- **Ground (off by default).** With fillets on and `FilletGroundK` > 0, the ground in the skirt field blends with the tree at that radius instead of `GroundBlend` (12 cm), and a ground skirt is built. The cull margin stays 90 cm, since a branch farther than ground + k cannot change the ground blend. 80 cm is the tried value (`Leaf.Fillets 1 -1 -1 -1 80`). The default is 0, because where a root meets the trunk at the ground the ground skirt and the root's junction skirt overlap and leave a lip (shots `ground-artifact-*`).
- **Leaves** are placed against a field without fillets (`PlainField` when the walking field has them, else the walking field), so leaf count and order do not depend on either flag (checked equal on seeds 517192, 377994 and 675856).
- **Skirts.** The mesh lays a skirt over every filleted junction and round the tree's foot (see Mesh). A junction whose skirt cannot be built loses its fillet, and the skirts are laid again (logged).
- **Drawn on the skirts** (with `bFilletWalk` off; with it on, only points laid on the field without fillets are lifted, such as a trail from an older save). The sim position lies under a skirt by up to the fillet's lift over the tubes (worst about 40 cm in the crease of a big limb), so what is drawn there is lifted onto the skirt field (`ATreeActor::LiftOntoSkirts`, display only): every ant's `VisualRoot` (meshes and the carried piece; the actor stays at the walking position, `UAntMovementComponent::GetShownPosition`), the trail ribbons (`FPath::Shown`, lifted once per point, again when the tree regrows) and the exposure spots. Nothing in the sim reads those positions. With fillets off nothing is lifted. The camera follows the drawn ant; the bark cutaway traces the skirt field, and opens by the ant's depth under it only where a lift failed.
- **Walking on them (14 Nov).** With the fillets in the walking field, laden recruits stuck at the same junctions every day (`Tools/tree_test.sh auto20`: 69 hauled in twenty days against 470 without, 37 rescues, `Saved/RefShots/bird-baseline/summary.md`), so for an afternoon the fillets were looks only. The cause was the follower, not the field (projections converged, error 0.00 cm, gradient length 0.95 to 1): the test walks (`BuildTestWalk`) leave route steps of 3 to 4 m whose straight line runs up to 42 cm inside the wood (at limb 6 of tree 1 one step goes from the limb's underside to its top). `TickFollow` held its point while the ant trailed it by more than 30 cm, and each one-second snap moved it on by one tick, about 1 cm/s, so 4 m took five minutes and the 420 s rescue came first (the coordinates repeated because that is where the clock ran out). Now, with fillets, the lag is counted to the bark above a buried point (lag minus its depth). auto20 with fillets walked: no rescues, 487.9 hauled against 481.9 by the same day lines without (same upgrades, milestones, moves and end card; days 3, 10, 15 and 18 differ by a recruit blown off or not); with `Leaf.Fillets 0` identical to the baseline line by line. The plain field has the same creep (a 295 cm step 37 cm deep on tree 1), short enough to get through; it is left as it was so fillets off stays identical. The player crossed root 1 and limb 6 onto the trunk, and walked both ways along both creases, with no stall and no projection failure (projection error at most 0.04 cm; stills `Saved/RefShots/fillet-walk/` in the fillet-walk worktree).
- Without fillets in the walking field (and for twigs) the old compromise holds: the tubes intersect with a crease while the field rounds it with the small `BlendK`, so the ant rides at most about k/4 above the crease (and under the skirt where there is one).

### Mesh (`TreeMeshBuilder`, `TreeActor`)

- Tubes around each centreline, rings of vertices, closed ends, built into a `UProceduralMeshComponent`. One section for wood. No collision on it.
- The component is transient and created on demand, never saved into the level. The actor's root is a plain scene component.
- Vertex data for the bark shader: UV0 = (arc length around, distance along the branch, both in cm), UV1 = (R cos θ, R sin θ) in cm, UV2 = the distance along split as (Hi, Lo), Hi = floor(along / 256) × 256, so both halves are exact in half floats and their sum interpolates linearly (UV0.y alone has 8 to 16 cm steps high on the trunk). UV3 = (bark radius, buried distance). The bark radius is the branch radius without the trunk flare and without branch collars (`FTreeBranch::BarkRadius`): `M_TreeBark2` builds its ridge cylinder from it, because a cylinder of the true radius sweeps radially through the ridge cells wherever the radius changes fast along the branch, and the flare read as paving stones. The buried distance is the signed distance in cm to the overlapping branch (a child's vertices against its parent; the parent's bark inside a child is no longer hidden at all since look pass 2, `bMaskParentInChild`: it cut visible holes three times, last a polygonal hole in a limb), unclamped in a half float, plus 0.2 × the radius of the branch it is buried in, 10000 where nothing overlaps; the bark hides UV3.y below -`BuriedDepth` (15 cm, `MPC_View`, `Leaf.Tune cam BuriedDepth`), so about 235 cm by the trunk and about 33 cm inside a 90 cm limb. Seen through the cutaway, that buried bark was the black wedge at junctions (look pass, 13 Nov). Two earlier versions opened holes in the owner's playtest: an 8-bit vertex-alpha encoding clamped to ±25 cm and then ±200 cm (trunk rings are up to 300 cm apart, and a clamped end value pulls the linearly interpolated cut outward on both surfaces), and masking the parent inside children whose base ring pokes out of the parent (big limbs, roots: their open end showed). Even unclamped, the distance interpolated across the 200 to 300 cm triangles of big branches dips far below the true one (the flare is bell-shaped and branches bend, so the solids are not convex): on the owner's tree a root lost visible bark up to about 200 cm "inside" the trunk until the depth reached 200 (a sweep by a Claude agent), hence the depth that scales with the radius. Vertex colour = (branch level, random per branch, height on tree, the old 8-bit buried value, no longer read). The overlap pass costs about 100 ms per tree build (logged with the mesh build).
- Roots' bark radius (`FTreeParams::RootBarkTaper`, `Leaf.RootBark`): a root narrows by about 0.17 cm per cm along near the trunk, faster than the bark's along compression (`ALONG` 0.13 in `bark2_bark.hlsl`), so a bark cylinder of its true radius swept across the plate cells and they broke into cobbles. Its bark radius is held near R_exit, the radius where it leaves the trunk's (flared) tube: R_exit + 0.15 (R - R_exit). Only UV3.x changes; the geometry, field and leaves are the same.
- Branch collars (`FTreeParams::bBranchCollars`, off by default since the owner saw them: they swell the child but leave the corner hard; the owner wants fillets, `Leaf.Collars 0|1` regrows today's tree): every limb, bough and root swells where it leaves its parent, by `CollarAmount` (0.45, boughs 0.8 of it) of its base radius, ramping in over the second half of its buried length (so the base ring stays inside the parent) and decaying over `CollarLength` (1.2) base radii. Applied to the skeleton radii after growth, drawing no random numbers, so the walking surface and the mesh agree and the tree is the same tree. Twigs get none: they carry leaves from 15% of their length, and leaf count and order must not change (one-tree saves store bites by leaf index); checked equal with collars on and off.
- **Junction skirts** (`Tree/TreeSkirts.inl`; the ant walks on them with `bFilletWalk`, under them without). They are built when `TreeMeshBuilder::Build` is given a field with fillets; the grove and `Leaf.MeshGap` build without one, so they get none.
  - **Extent.** Each filleted junction gets a band from the child's first ring clear of the blend, over the fillet, to a curve on the parent where the field is 1.1 fillet radii from the child.
  - **Construction.** The band's columns are the child ring's vertices. Each column is a sweep of rays from a point on the child's axis inside both tubes (their union is star-shaped about it). The rays are solved on the two tubes blended as the field blends them, then each point is moved onto the whole field. A point deep inside a later branch crossing the skirt (one of the child's boughs) stays where it is, hidden. A skirt whose points would have to move more than 8 cm is given up.
  - **Edges.** Inner rows sit 0.3 cm outside the field. The first row is the ring itself; the last lies on the parent's facets, with the parent's interpolated vertex data. So nothing steps where the skirt meets the tubes.
  - **Bark data.** The child half carries the ring's bark data continued down the band (UV1, bark radius, along decreasing with the distance travelled); the parent half carries the parent's. They switch with a hard seam where the surface is equally far from both tubes, where the crease was. UV3.y = 10000, alpha 1.
  - **Seam cross-fade data** (`leaf.SkirtBlend`, with `Leaf.Bark 2`). Near the seam each skirt vertex also carries the other branch's bark: UV0 = its radial direction × its bark radius, UV3.y = its along + 512 (stays above -BuriedDepth, so the cutaway's buried test keeps it), colour R = its per-branch random, alpha = 1 - the weight. The weight is 0.5 on the seam row of both halves and eases to 0 over 85% of each half's arc length; tube alpha is 1 (their old buried alpha was read by no bark material). M_TreeBark2 reads none of these (a paused pixel diff with and without is at frame noise). M_TreeBark3 (`Tools/materials/bark3_bark.hlsl`, built by `build_bark3_mcp.py` as a duplicate of M_TreeBark2) evaluates the bark a second time with the other branch's coordinates where the weight is above 0 and height-blends the two (the higher plate shows), symmetric so both halves agree on the seam row; it also darkens the bark into the soil over its last 30 to 50 cm above the ground plane. Logged per tree: `TreeSkirts blend: ... seam row mismatch ...` (0 in doubles; after the GPU's half-float UVs about 0.3 cm in bark coordinates and up to 8 cm in along on the tallest branches, about 1 cm in the plates' compressed space).
  - **Tubes unchanged.** The tubes and their buried-bark data are as before; the crease lies behind the skirt.
  - **Console variables.** `leaf.SkirtSeam 1` puts the switch at the outer edge instead (tried: the child's bark fans out over the parent and the outer edge shows; worse). `leaf.Skirts 0` builds no skirts, for comparison.
  - **Cost.** Built in parallel, 50 to 100 ms per tree, about 60k vertices.
- **Ground skirt** (same file; only when `FilletGroundK` > 0, off by default). It covers the tree's outline at ground level (the tree alone, by marching squares on a 25 cm grid), one column every 30 cm.
  - **Columns.** Each column is a sweep of rays across the outline, solved on the whole field, from a point in the corner inside the tree below the ground. The sweep runs from the wall 1.1 × 80 cm up, over the fillet, to the ground 1.1 × 80 cm out.
  - **Tree half.** It carries the bark of the nearest tube (trunk or root), interpolated as that tube does, and goes into the bark section. Its top row is on the tube's facets where it meets a plain tube.
  - **Ground half.** It goes into the ground's own section with the ground's vertex data (UV0 = world XY, black). Its last row lies on the ground plane. The material switches from bark to `M_Ground` with a hard seam where the surface is equally far from the tree and the ground; there is no fade, since the bark shader has no fade.
  - **Lift.** Inner rows sit 0.5 cm out, above the junction skirts at root-trunk corners; junction skirts are not moved onto the field within 1.5 × the ground blend + 10 cm of the ground.
  - **Nest.** Columns within the mound radius + 130 cm + 1.2 × 80 cm of the nest spot are left out (none on the test seeds; the skirt stays 414 cm or more from the nest centre).
  - **Gaps.** Runs of failed columns are filled from their neighbours (`leaf.GroundFill 1`), and the columns at the strip's open ends are laid onto the wall and the ground (`leaf.GroundClose 1`); 0 leaves the gap or the raised edge, for comparison. `leaf.GroundSkirt 0` builds no ground skirt.
  - **Cost.** 15 to 25 ms.
- `M_TreeBark2` (look pass, 13 Nov; the default until 15 Nov, now `M_TreeBark3` with `Leaf.Bark 2`; `Leaf.Bark 0` switches back to `M_TreeBark`, loaded by path so a missing asset falls back). Three Custom HLSL nodes whose code lives in `Tools/materials/bark2_*.hlsl` (the scripts there rebuild or update the material over unreal-mcp; never edit the code only in the editor). Bark space q = (direction round the branch × bark radius (UV3.x), along × 0.13): plates are Voronoi cells of q, about 14 cm across and eight times longer along the branch (look pass 2: 0.085 read as carved wood, ridges that never ended), warped so furrows wander; furrows are their soft borders (0.3 of a cell wide, 1.8 cm deep), darker wood (0.42 of the ridge colour) with no outline, widened with the pixel footprint so they never alias. Relief is the analytic gradient of the same height (normal only). Each ridge has its own tone; mottling, a slow tone change across the tree, fine grain streaks up close (gone by about 2.5 m), roughness from 0.74 (ridges) to 0.95 (furrows), AO in the furrows. Moss in patches in true proportions, a little more in furrows, on top of limbs and low down. Twigs are smoother and warmer. Colour and moss noise use (UV1, along) unstretched. A layer of small ridged-noise cracks was tried and dropped: it drew looping outlines. The "triangular seams" of the first version were not mesh data (debug views of every interpolated input were smooth) but the relief: the furrow slope is the gradient of F2 − F1, which jumps where the second-nearest cell point switches, and wide furrows put those switch lines on visible slopes. Moss is olive grey, darker and duller than any leaf (leaf colour is information). `DEBUG_VIEW` in `bark2_bark.hlsl` shows the inputs as colours.
- `M_TreeBark` (the fallback) evaluates 3D noise at (UV1, 0.15 × along + 5000 × random): a cylinder around each branch, so there is no seam around, features keep their physical size, tips close to a point, and fissures run along the branch. On the steep root flare the stretch is lost (the radius changes faster than 0.15 per cm), so the flare reads as plates. UVs are half precision, a few cm of step high on the trunk, which the long fissures hide. `M_Bark` is the older UV-based version, kept as a fallback.
- **Bark relief (planned for the presentation milestone; not built, the bark's relief is shader normals only).** Add one analytic ridge function `R(angle, along)` to both the field (`D -= R`) and the mesh vertices, so the fissures in the concept art are real geometry the ant walks over. Keep it gentle: height up to 0.5 AL, wavelength at least 3 AL. Ring density on the trunk must resolve it (about 8 vertices per ridge). Finer detail is shader normals only. Build the smooth version first.
- **Verify**: lighting on a procedural mesh. It has no distance field, so Lumen software tracing ignores it. The project has hardware ray tracing and virtual shadow maps enabled. Check early with a screenshot that the tree casts and receives shadows and is not flat-lit; fall back to screen traces plus a strong sky light if hardware tracing misbehaves.

### Leaves (`LeafSet`)

```
FLeaf { FVector Base; FQuat Rot; float Length; int32 Twig; float Remaining; uint8 Bites; FVector2f BiteUV[3]; float BiteR[3]; }
```

- Leaves attach by their base to twigs. Thousands of them; all are data, a spatial hash finds the ones near an ant.
- Rendered as quads in procedural mesh sections, chunked per limb so one cut updates one small section.
- Shape, veins and serration come from UV maths in a masked, two-sided foliage material. Backlit glow matters for the look.
- Bites: up to three circles per leaf, passed in spare UV channels and vertex colour, discarded in the shader. Updating a bite rewrites that chunk's vertex data.
- Wind sway by world position offset, driven by a material parameter collection.

## Ant

### Movement (`AntMovementComponent`)

A plain `APawn` with a custom movement update. **No `ACharacter`, no physics, no capsule.**

```
State: Pos, Up, Forward, Velocity (tangent to the surface)

Tick(dt):
  n       = Field.Gradient(Pos)
  wish    = camera-relative input, projected onto the plane with normal n
            (if the camera looks along n, use the camera's up as "forward")
  Velocity= approach(Velocity, wish * Speed, Accel * dt), kept tangent to n
  Pos    += Velocity * dt
  Field.Project(Pos, n, RideHeight)
  Up      = slerp(Up, n, UpRate * dt)
  Forward = slerp toward Velocity direction when moving
```

- Substep when `Speed * dt` exceeds half an ant length, so thin twigs are not skipped.
- The ant's origin rides `RideHeight` (1.5 cm) above the field; the mesh and the carry socket are drawn `MeshSinkCm` (1.2 cm) lower, so the feet touch the bark (playtest, 13 Nov). Visual only: routes and trail points keep `RideHeight`. The ant mesh is `SM_Ant2` (with a neck joining head and thorax; `SM_Ant` is the fallback).
- **Controls: screen direction everywhere** (`FAntMoveTuning::ControlStyle` 0, the default since 13 Nov, owner's design). The stick (or W/A/S/D) is a direction on screen. When it is first pushed, `MakeScreenBasis` turns "up" and "right" on screen into two surface directions (`HeldFwd`, `HeldRight`); while the stick is held they are carried along the bark with the velocity (rotated by the change of surface normal each substep), so a held stick keeps going straight over the surface and the camera is free meanwhile (it no longer steers). The camera is read again after the stick has been centred for `PauseSeconds` (0.05 s since 14 Nov; it was 0.15 s and the owner found the wait odd), or when the stick direction jumps by more than `NewInputAngleDeg` (40 degrees) from one frame to the next; gradual stick turns steer within the held directions. With `bHeldFollowsLook` (off by default, the owner's trial: `Leaf.Tune move bHeldFollowsLook True`) the held directions are also read again every frame in which the player turns the camera (mouse or right stick, `UAntMovementComponent::SetPlayerLooking` from the controller), so W follows a deliberate look at once while the camera's own roll and follow still cannot change it. **On a branch** (any level, the trunk too) the stick is read against how the whole branch lies on screen, not the patch of bark under the ant: the branch axis is projected to the screen with perspective at the ant (the camera position comes with `SetMoveInput`; away from the camera moves a point toward the screen centre), plus `AxisDepthWeight` (0.25) of "away counts as up" for a branch pointing straight at the camera. Stick along that screen direction walks along the branch; across it turns the branch's side facing the camera that way (round the branch); diagonals go diagonally. On the ground: screen-up (`CamUp + ScreenDepthWeight * CamForward`) and screen-right projected onto the surface. Going round a branch is limited to `MaxLapsPerSecond` (1) laps a second, walking along it is not, so a diagonal on a twig is a gentle spiral. `bSnapOnBranches` (off by default, owner's choice) snaps the held direction to along or around on thin wood (`SnapFullRadius` 25 cm, fading by `SnapFadeRadius` 60 cm). The rounded tip keeps the old latch (stop at the apex, leave when the stick reverses).
- **Old controls** (`ControlStyle` 1, `Leaf.Tune move ControlStyle 1`): camera-relative on the trunk and the ground, and on wood under `WheelFullRadius` (fading by `WheelFadeRadius`) the steering wheel: W/S along the branch, A/D round it; which end W means (`WheelAway`) is picked from the camera at press (or from the ant's facing when the branch runs across the screen) and kept until release. Below about 70 cm a gentle stick (under 0.2) used the even older thin-branch rules. Playtest (13 Nov): A/D circling on a branch that runs across the screen, W flipping after circling a branch, and the switch of meaning at junctions were the reasons for the change.
- **Input view** (`Leaf.InputViz`, F3 while `bInputVizKey` is True in `DefaultGame.ini`): short paths over the bark for where W, A, S and D would go from here (`PreviewWish`, the same rules without changing any state), the live stick (white), the held direction before the twig snap (thin cyan), the actual movement (green), and the rule in charge, the branch radius, the snapshot state and the stick on the left of the screen. Always in the log (player's ant, dev builds): `Control trace: onto branch ...` for every junction crossing with the stick held (radius and rule before and after, how far forward moved on screen), and `Control trace: moving N deg off the stick's screen direction ...` when the ant moves more than 60 degrees away from the stick's screen direction for a third of a second (circling a twig behind it shows here by design).
- States: `Walking`, `Cutting` (rooted), `Gripping` (rooted), `Riding` (following a trail), `Returning` (unattended walk home: from the nearby scent line point nearest the nest, along that line with its loops cut out; `ATrailNetwork::BuildReturnRoute`), `Falling` (simple ballistic drop to the ground, then `WalkingHome` along the ground to the nest).
- Sprint multiplies speed and drains stamina. Carried mass scales speed down.
- The same component drives the player's ant and unattended ants; only the source of "wish" differs.

### Camera (`AntCamera`)

A separate actor that follows the controlled ant. It is never attached.

- Keeps its own reference up `CamUp`, which eases toward the ant's `Up` with a time constant of about 0.6 s. **Tune.**
- Yaw and pitch orbit around `CamUp`. When `CamUp` changes, keep the view direction as steady as possible and re-orthogonalise.
- The desired position is behind and above the ant at a boom length; the actual position follows with a critically damped spring.
- Wood does not push the camera in (`bAvoidThickOcclusion` restores the old boom shortening). The camera passes through branches; the bark dithers away within `CutawayRadius` of the line from the camera to the ant, narrowing to nothing at the ant, and anything near the camera. The camera publishes its position and look-at point to `MPC_View` (`CamPos`, `AntFocus`, `CutawayOn`, `CutawayRadius`, and for `M_TreeBark2` also `CutawayBand` (rim width as a fraction of the radius), `CutawayTaper` (radius × (1 − t)^taper), `NearFadeStart`/`NearFadeEnd` (cm from the camera), `BuriedDepth`, `BarkCut`) every frame, so the cutaway tunes live with `Leaf.Tune cam`. Faces are one-sided, so from inside a limb the limb itself is not drawn; what showed through was bark buried in the neighbouring branch, now hidden (see the mesh section).
- The bark's cutaway opens only when wood really hides the ant (look pass 2): each frame the camera sphere-traces the line to the ant (ending 10 cm short of it) through the tree field and measures how deep the line runs through wood; `BarkCut` goes from 0 (clear) to 1 at `CutawayDepthFull` (25 cm), eased in at `CutawayEaseIn` (8/s) and out at `CutawayEaseOut` (3/s), and scales only the bark's cutaway radius, so the near-camera fade still works. Before, the bark dug a trench under the line whenever the camera was low over the surface the ant stood on. Leaves keep `CutawayOn` (they are not in the field). `Leaf.CutLog 1` logs depth and factor every 0.25 s.
- Look-up assist (`bLookUpAssist`, off by default): when pitching up would put the camera under the ant's own surface, the camera comes in along the view line to `LookUpClearance` above that surface (no nearer than `LookUpMinBoom`) and looks along the pitched view, turned back toward the ant only as far as needed to keep it near the bottom of the frame. Depth of field focuses on the ant's real distance. `ExposureMinEV`/`ExposureMaxEV` fix the auto exposure range when set (both -100 leave the level's settings). Shadows ignore the cutaway (`ShadowReplace`). `M_Leaf` (masked since 12 Nov) does the same for leaves in a Custom HLSL node, at 1.5 times the radius and without the narrowing, but it keeps leaves within 15 to 45 cm of the ant (the leaf being cut is usually there); leaves within 25 to 45 cm of the camera fade too. The camera never goes below `GroundClearance` above the ground, and never sits inside wood thicker than `ThickWoodRadius` (it is projected out to `WoodClearance` above the bark and looks at the ant from there).
- Shifting ants is a deliberate one-second glide to the new ant, not a cut.
- Shallow depth of field focused on the ant sells the macro scale. Keep it mild enough that the route ahead stays readable.

### Visual (`AntPawn`)

- Fallback when neither `SM_Ant2` nor `SM_Ant` loads: three engine spheres.
- The mesh: `SM_Ant2`, a static mesh from `docs/briefs/ant-mesh.md` (see Movement). Legs animate in the material from a gait phase and walk speed supplied by C++ through a dynamic material instance.
- A named socket or fixed offset at the mandibles holds the carried fragment, which is a small procedural leaf piece scaled by its size and held overhead.
- **Carried piece look** (`Leaf.PieceVeins`, default on): `AAntPawn::BuildFragmentMesh` gives the piece the UVs and vertex colours of the leaf it was cut from (`FragmentLeaf`, `FragmentBite`, set by every cut path before `SetFragment`): UV = (along / length, 0.5 + across / width) of that leaf, with the piece's straight side at 70% of the half-width (not on the margin itself, where `M_Leaf` fades the side veins; big pieces reach the midrib); colour = (1, that leaf's per-leaf random, height, its shape richness ±0.1 in soft lobes across the piece). `M_Leaf` is unchanged. A piece handed over by `Leaf.Carry` borrows the nearest leaf within 6 m, else keeps the old fixed look (UVs of a 2 m leaf near its margin, richness 0). Built once per cut: no frame-time cost.

## Trails (`TrailNetwork`)

- Each worker owns `TArray<FTrailPoint> Path` (`Pos`, `Normal`), sampled every 0.5 AL of travel from the moment it leaves the nest, whether it walked or rode.
- **Returning** is that array played backwards at the ant's laden speed, each point re-projected onto the field.
- The network keeps every path for the day. For drawing, a new path only adds geometry where it is farther than a threshold from existing trail; where it overlaps, it raises that trail's strength.
- **Riding**: find the nearest trail point within reach, then advance along that path away from the nest. At the end of a path, or on any movement input, return to `Walking`. Where paths fork, prefer the one whose direction best matches the camera's forward.
- **Yesterday's trails** (one tree per stage): at day end `ExportToday` hands the day's paths to the save. The next day `LoadYesterday` adds them as paths flagged `bYesterday`: drawn at `YesterdayGlow` (0.3), 0.4 cm lower than today's ribbon, never brightened by lines, ignored by today's merge test (a fresh trail over a faint one draws at full strength), left out of `DrawnLength`, and ridden and followed home like any other path. They are dropped when that day ends (the save then holds the newer day's routes) and on a move.
- Rendering: a thin ribbon lifted slightly off the surface along the stored normals, unlit translucent cyan. Brightness (vertex alpha, which `M_Trail` multiplies into opacity) shows each line's patch: `ATrailNetwork::SetLineGlow` gives every drawn stretch within `MergeDistance` of a line's route the brightest glow of the lines using it (full patch 1.0, stripped 0.3, no line 0.25), and only the chunks that changed are rebuilt. Faint stretches are a little thinner.

## The line (`LineSystem`)

Spec: `docs/briefs/the-line.md`. `ALineSystem` (spawned by the game mode, initialised with the tree, trails, nest, wind and director) owns lines, the recruit pool and the fixed-step clock.

- **Line**: made by `AddLine` when a scout's piece is on its way home (`ADayDirector::SendHome`, or a scout carrying it into the nest). The route is `BuildReturnRoute` from the scout's cut (`AAntPawn::GetLastCut`, recorded at the cut, not where send-home was pressed), nest first. The patch is `ATreeActor::BuildPatch` around the cut leaf (same twig, then neighbouring twigs of the bough, nearest first along the bark; `FLineTuning::PatchMaxLeaves` 4, `PatchMaxPieces` 10 including the scout's piece), and each patch leaf gets an approach route over the branch graph (`ATreeActor::BuildLeafApproach`). Leaves without an approach are left out.
- **Recruits** are `AAntPawn`s with `MakeRecruit`: never possessed, no trail recording, stepped by the line system (`SetSimDriven`, `SimStep`), spawned per trip and gone into the nest at the end (`DepositAndVanish`). States: `Nest`, `Queued`, `Out` (riding the line plus the approach at `OutSpeed`), `Cutting` (`StartAutoCut`, the template size at the ant's cut rate), `Laden` / `Empty` (route backwards at the laden speed × `HomeSpeedScale`), `Fallen` (blown off: fall, walk home over the ground). Per-leaf reservations keep two recruits from going for the last piece. A recruit not at its leaf `ReachTimeout` s after the end of the line cuts from where it stands (logged as a fallback). A rescue after `RescueAfter` s guarantees every recruit gets home.
- **Fixed steps**: `ALineSystem::Tick` accumulates real time × `SpeedFactor` and runs steps of `FLineTuning::Step` (1/120 s): the wind clock (`AWindSystem::Advance`; the wind's own tick only does sound, materials and streaks), every recruit and, during the harvest, returning scouts handed over with `AddDrivenAnt`. A frame budget (`FrameBudgetMs`) bounds the skip; over budget the backlog is dropped, so results never depend on frame rate or on watching versus skipping. Transforms and leg animation are pushed once per frame (`PushVisuals`).
- **Values**: `FTreeLeaf::Richness` (1× low to 3× high and far out, times the stage's `RichnessMul`; the height share builds up over the first `FDayTuning::RichnessHeightScale` metres above the lowest leaf, 40 by default since 15 Nov, so a layer or two up is worth about 1.5× to 1.8×; 0 gives the older linear curve over the crown) multiplies a piece's value (`AAntPawn::GetCarriedValue`); the carried weight that slows the ant stays size-based. Leaf vertex alpha carries `(Richness / RichnessMul - 1) / 2` for `M_Leaf` (the shape-based value, so a stage's richness multiplier does not saturate the look).
- **Read-outs**: `GetLines` (per line: pieces home and lost, patch remaining, fullness, recruits on it), `NumIdle`, `GetPatchPreview` (cached a second), `GetRecentDeliveries` (for the "+N"; the chime pitch rises with `Chain`).

- Each line has its own colour, in order per day: `FLineTuning::LineColors` (`Leaf.Tune line LineColors`), cyan, magenta-pink, violet, blue, ice white, repeating (no green, amber or red: those mean exposure). `SetLineGlow` colours the stretches it brightens: the brightest line takes a shared stretch (the lower-numbered on a tie); trails on no line and yesterday's keep the old cyan (`NoLineColor`). The colour is the ribbon's vertex RGB divided by `ATrailNetwork::ColorGain` (1.1); `M_Trail` and `M_TrailSmoke` show vertex RGB x 1.1 as emissive (alpha stays the brightness). The HUD's line rows and recap rows show each label (and a stripe) in its line's colour; the patch read-out names and colours the line it would become ("Line 3 here: ...").
- Debug: `-LeafTrailVC` on the command line draws the trails with the engine's vertex colour material (checks the colours without the trail materials).

## Cutting and carrying

- When the controlled ant is within reach of a leaf base, show the prompt.
- Holding the cut button enters `Cutting`. Fragment size grows at a rate set by the Mandibles upgrade, up to the lesser of the size cap and the leaf's remaining area.
- The predicted risk colour comes from the wind formula below, using today's full gust strength and an exposure of 1.
- On release: write the bite into the leaf, spawn the fragment on the ant, set carried mass.
- **Send home** switches the ant to `Returning` and asks the roster for the next worker.
- Reaching the nest while carrying deposits the mass into today's haul.

## Wind (`WindSystem`)

- One prevailing direction per day (horizontal, random per day), with a few degrees of wander.
- Phases: `Calm`, `Warning`, `Rising`, `Holding`, `Falling`. Durations and peak strength scale with the day number; with one tree per stage, `StartDay(Day, Seed, ForceScale, RampDay[, Yaw])` takes peak force `PeakForceDay1 × stage GustMul × (1 + GustPerDayOnTree × (days on this tree − 1))` and ramps the gust cycle by days on the tree, seeded from hash(tree seed, day) so the direction changes daily (a `Yaw` of 0 or more fixes the direction; the random draw still happens, so gust timing is unchanged).
- Publishes `Strength` (0–1) and `Direction` to a material parameter collection for leaf sway, streaks and the audio mix.
- Exposure at a point: `Facing = saturate(dot(Normal, -WindDir) * 0.5 + 0.5)`, multiplied by `Open`, which is 0.25 if `Field.March` upwind hits the tree within a few metres and 1 otherwise.
- Blow-off test, evaluated a few times per second during `Rising` and `Holding`:

```
Force = Strength * PeakForce(day) * Exposure * (BodyArea + FragmentArea)
Hold  = BaseGrip * GripUpgrade * (Gripping ? GripBonus : 1)
blown off when Force > Hold
```

- Streaks are math-only: ribbons or a Niagara system stretched along the wind direction. They appear in `Warning` so the player has time to react.

### In-world wind cues (`WindCues`)

Brief: `docs/briefs/in-world-cues.md`. `AWindCues` (World/WindCues.*) is spawned by `AWindSystem::BeginPlay` and only reads the wind (phase, time left, strength, gust peak, direction, `GetExposure` at the controlled ant five times a second); it changes nothing in the wind, exposure, walking field or economy. Behind `Leaf.Cues` (default on); with it off, `AWindSystem::UpdateStreaks` draws its old streaks unchanged and the grip pose is the old one; with it on, the old streaks are cleared and `AWindCues` draws all streaks. Tuning: the `Cue*` fields of `FWindTuning` (`Leaf.Tune wind`). Nothing is drawn while the wind's cues are muted (the skipped harvest); while the watched harvest fast-forwards (line system speed above 1.5) there is no front, wall or warning stir, only streaks following the wind's strength, as before (a front would flash past every few seconds). Its random stream is its own, so gust timing never depends on it.

- **Gust front.** From the warning on, a front moves downwind at `CueFrontSpeed` (300 cm/s, so it starts about 9 m upwind) and reaches the controlled ant exactly as the gust starts rising (it stands `CueFrontSpeed` × time left upwind of the ant; `Leaf.CuesLog` shows it). Air upwind of it shows wind strength `CueFrontLevel` × the gust's peak; ahead of it the wind's own strength, raised during the warning by `CuePrecursor` × that level × the warning's progress (an all-round stir, for a camera that has the front behind it). Every streak and speck takes its visibility, width, length and speed from the air at its own position, so streaks thicken, lengthen and speed up on the upwind side first and the change sweeps past; specks gather in a band at the front (`CueFrontDust`). Visibility starts at each particle's own threshold, so calm air draws no streaks (the calm motes, `CueCalmMotes` 0.03, are nearly invisible).
- **Dust wall.** The front carries `CueFrontPuffs` big faint puffs (radius `CueFrontPuffSize`, alpha `CueFrontHaze` 0.13, subtler since the playtest), each three nested soft quads (one quad of `M_Exposure` has a firm rim and read as a blob). Half are light (`CueFrontPuffColor`) and half dark (`CueFrontPuffDark`): thin streaks far away barely show against a bright sky and a light haze vanishes there, so the dark half carries the wall over sky and the light half over bark and ground (measured against the cues-off pass: 10 to 24 grey levels darker over sky, 18 to 47 brighter over dark ground). The wall fades in over the warning's first 0.6 s, out over the rise, and within `CueFrontPuffNear` of the camera, so it never fogs the view as it passes. While it is up, auto exposure dims the scene by a few levels.
- **Pools.** Streaks (`CueStreaks`, 240) carry the gust: they fill the view out to `CueFar` and are thin curved strips (`StripRows` 6 points: a gentle arc across the wind of `CueStreakCurve` × the length plus a slow S-wave), tapered and fading in and out along their length, faster than the old streaks (`CueStreakSpeed`). Specks are only a few warm motes (`CueSpecks` 60, `CueLeafBitShare` of them tumbling leaf bits) out to `CueSpeckFar`, more of them near, each always a short streak along its motion (at least four times as long as wide, `CueStretch` × its speed): the owner's playtest of the first version (14 Nov, 280 cream-white specks) read it as a snowstorm. Particles leaving the view are respawned in it. Nothing is drawn thinner than `CueMinPixels`, and nothing nearer the camera than `CueStreakNear` / `CueSpeckNear` / `CueLocalNear` (fading in over the second half).
- **The ant's air.** `CueLocal` particles (70: about half short streak strips, a few motes, the rest specks) are aimed past the controlled ant from `CueLocalRadius` upwind, through a disc across the wind of radius `CueLocalSpread` round a point `CueLocalLift` above the ant (biased to its axis; where the disc dips under the bark it is mirrored above). Near the ant the flow is the wind without the part leaving the bark, so on a lee face it runs along the bark rather than out of it. Specks that strike the bark within the disc slide along it, one way (across the face where the wind hits square on). Where the ant is sheltered, a calm pocket of radius `CueShelterRadius` × (1 − smoothstep(0.12, 0.32, exposure)) sits on it (full below the HUD's sheltered line, gone soon above it, where the HUD says to grip): everything drawn near it is pushed out from the flow axis so it streams round (each point of a strip on its own, so streaks bend round it) (roughly the streamlines round a sphere; never into the bark), streaks through it fade, and motes hang still inside (`CueMoteAlpha`). On exposed bark there is no pocket and specks rush straight past the ant, or strike the windward bark round it and slide on.
- **Grip.** At the grip `CueGripPuff` grains burst out along the bark all round the ant, half warm tan and half dark (so they show on pale boughs and dark trunk bark); while the wind pulls (air at the ant × exposure), `CueGripTrickle` per second more are blown downwind. The gripping pose (`AAntPawn::UpdateCutVisuals` calls `WindCues::GripPose` while the cues are on) presses the body flatter and wider (`CueGripSquash` 0.62, `CueGripSpread` 1.3; before 0.78 and 1.14), sinks it `CueGripSink` into the bark (the feet dig in), dips it briefly as the claws dig in, tips the head down 3 degrees, and tugs it downwind with the wind it feels (`CueGripTug`). From above the burst reads; the pose reads mostly from the side.
- **Leaves.** `M_Leaf` sways a leaf's tip by 20 cm × `MPC_Wind.WindStrength` along its normal (× U0², the stalk end fixed) on a fixed 2 s sine; it is the only reader of `WindStrength`. On 2 m leaves 20 cm at a full gust barely showed, so with the cues on `AWindSystem::Tick` writes `WindStrength` × (1 + `CueLeafBoost` × `WindStrength`²), at most `CueLeafMax` (1.5 and 3: calm 0.12 stays 0.12, a full gust gives 2.5, a 50 cm tip swing, about 14 degrees; nothing folds through its twig). With the cues off it writes the strength as before. Measured on the canopy view (frames 1 s apart in a gust, mean grey-level change of the frame): 11 to 14 before, 16 to 23 with 1.5, 21 to 25 at the cap. The sway is still slow (2 s); a quicker gust-only shiver needs the material: `Tools/materials/leaf_flutter.hlsl` (optional, not built).
- **Drawing.** One procedural mesh, two sections of camera-facing quads with fixed vertex counts (unused quads collapse to a point), updated every frame: streaks with `M_Exposure` (a soft ellipse from UV, vertex colour; `CueStreakMaterial` 1 uses `M_WindStreak`) and specks, puffs and grains with `M_Exposure`. A strip has UV (±1, 0), so the disc softens it across its width only; its ends fade by vertex alpha. `M_Exposure` is unlit and bright: vertex colour 0.15 still draws near white in daylight, so colours are low: streaks `CueStreakColor` (0.5 grey, the one light element: wind lines), specks warm and dark (`CueSpeckColor` ochre 0.13 / 0.095 / 0.035, `CueSpeckColor2` dust, `CueLeafBitColor` olive brown). Nothing is near-white except the streaks, and nothing uses the HUD's green / amber / red.
- **Seen from where.** The front reads best with the wind across the screen or toward the camera (it comes in from the upwind side, the side the HUD's wind arrow points away from). With the camera facing downwind the front comes from behind it; then only the all-round stir shows during the warning, which is weak.
- **Cost.** `Leaf.CuesStat` over a forced gust (editor binaries, 240 six-point streak strips, 60 specks, the ant's 70, 36 × 3 dust-wall quads): 0.23 ms of game thread on average, single frames up to about 0.75 ms (another Unreal process shared the machine). The 0.3 ms budget holds on average, not for every frame; 300 seven-point strips cost 0.30 ms. The first version's `perfbase` A/B (cues off / on, forced gusts, 60 Hz cap) showed no difference beyond noise: game thread 1.98 / 2.03 ms, GPU 3.20 / 3.21 ms.

### Video recording

`Game/LeaflineShots.*` is a transient actor created only by a video command. Its commands live in `Game/LeaflineVideoCommands.cpp`, available in Development packages. The actor runs before `AAntCamera` in `TG_PostPhysics`, so the camera manager reads the current frame's pose. External drive freezes the player's orbit state and restores transform, FOV and the complete post-process settings on completion or cancellation. The restored view is held for one camera tick before ordinary following resumes. No recording code calls save or economy methods. Cinematic mode is independent of shots; ordinary play retains the existing tuning and creates no shot actor.

| Shot | Move | Horizontal FOV |
|---|---|---|
| orbit | One turn in the ant's surface tangent plane, at the usual boom with an outward lift | 65° |
| crane | Establish the ant in the lower third before rising behind it and tilting into the crown | 72° |
| dolly | Approach from 1.8 to 1 normal boom length along the branch, 45 cm above bark, ant in the lower third; ground uses a straight approach | 58° |
| reveal | Pull back from beside the nest toward an end view chosen from 12 away-side azimuths (fewest grove trees in front of the tree), at one-third tree height with the ground-to-crown span filling 70% of the frame height; the path is the tested 8 s move cut at its 6 s pose, eased across the whole duration with no hold; zero roll throughout | 65° opening, height-dependent finish |
| follow | Smooth the live ant position and heading for a chase from behind and above, including walking, trail rides and falls | 68° |

Quintic easing brings each move to rest at its ends. Every position is checked against the tree/ground field with 12 cm clearance (the normal camera's probe radius), projected outward if needed, and swept from the previous position. A blocked move slides along the local surface or holds at the last safe position. Other shots use an eased return: direct when clear, retracing recorded clear positions when obstructed. Reveal moves until completion (log `Reveal timing: step-4 path trimmed ...`), then restores the exact saved player view as a cut; it has no return animation. A starting player view already inside wood is rejected because returning to it would be unsafe. Completion logs report minimum clearance, held/clipped frames, and transform/FOV return errors. Watch clipped frames in a recording: geometric safety does not establish good framing or motion.

### Bird (`BirdSystem`)

- One actor, `ABirdSystem`, spawned by the game mode and stepped by `ALineSystem::StepAnts` after the ants (`Advance`), like the wind clock. Everything that decides anything runs there: the schedule, the choice of target and the hit. The visible bird in `Tick` only follows that state (it never feeds back), so a skipped harvest and a watched one stay the same, and the bird does nothing outside the `Shift` phase.
- Its own `FRandomStream`, seeded from hash(tree seed, day): the wind's gusts are the same with the bird on and off. Nothing is saved.
- **When:** only in `Shift`. Before the first shift (title, move card) it waits; once the phase leaves `Shift` (harvest, walk-home fast-forward, recap) it leaves for the day, and a dive in progress is cancelled ("Bird: dive at ... cancelled"). A bird day is a day with recruits on from `FirstBirdDayRecruits` (2) while the pool is above zero (a new colony has two recruits from day 1, and day 1 stays calm), or with recruits off from `FirstBirdDay` (3). The gentle first bird day (`FirstDaySwoops`, 1, and the hint) follows from the day and the pool alone, never from session history: with recruits off it is `FirstBirdDay`; with recruits on it is day 2, whatever Brood was bought after day 1, and any later day while the pool is still the smallest on the Brood ladder (2). A day whose budget is 0 has no bird. The day's remaining swoops survive `Leaf.Bird 0` / `1` (a toggle never buys more).
- **Cycle:** `Circling` (for `SwoopInterval` ± `SwoopJitter`, seeded) → looks for a target (none: looks again every `LookAgain` s) → `Diving` (`WarnSeconds`, following the target) → pass → `Away` (`AwaySeconds`, `BaitAwaySeconds` after taking bait) → `Circling`, at most `MaxSwoopsPerDay` (2; the brief started at 3, which moved the end card a day later) a day; after the last it does not come back.
- **Requests:** `Leaf.BirdNow` and the drop button only set a request; it is taken at the start of the next fixed step, so its target and exposure are decided in the sim like everything else.
- **Target:** only candidates the bird has a clear way in to (`FindApproach`); a dropped piece on exposed bark first (tier 2), else among laden ants in a state a gust can blow off (walking, riding, returning, cutting, gripping; scouts by roster index, then recruits by pool index), the one with the highest `AWindSystem::GetExposure`, if it is in the exposed band (`World/ExposureBands.h`: exposed from 0.6, sheltered below 0.25; the HUD and the wind map read the same header).
- **Way in:** at the dive's start the schedule picks the corridor the bird comes in along (`ChooseApproach`: from the pass point, `PassGap` out from the target's bark, along the first clear one of between-normal-and-upwind, upwind, the normal, and those tilted up; `Field.March` with 70 cm near the bark and up to 250 cm further out). It checks that corridor again 20 times a second from where the target now is; if wood blocks it (the target went round a branch), the bird breaks off: a miss, it counts as a swoop, and the visible bird pulls back out the way it came.
- **Pass:** the target is tested again where it stands: not exposed any more → missed; gripping → holds; otherwise `AAntPawn::BlowOff(..., EBlowOffCause::Bird)` (log: "knocked off by the bird"), the existing fall, piece flutter, roster and recruit callbacks. Other laden ants within `HitRadius` (120 cm) get the same test until `MaxHitsPerSwoop` (2) are knocked off. The bird counts its own hits (`GetPiecesTaken`, `GetRecruitPiecesTaken`) for the recap line "Pieces lost to the bird: N"; the recap's "lost to the wind" for recruits subtracts the bird's. Each day logs "Bird: day N summary: swoops, knocked off, held, missed, dropped pieces taken". The player's own scout moves and grips on the frame tick, not in the fixed steps (as for gusts): the pass reads its position and grip as they are at that step, so its outcome can differ by a frame between runs; recruits and returning scouts are fully in the fixed steps.
- **Visible bird:** `SM_Bird` with a MID of `M_Bird` (scalars `FlapPhase`, radians, and `FlapAmount`, 0 gliding to 1 flapping) if the asset exists, else a dark stretched engine sphere. `SM_Bird` is real size (1 m wingspan, 62 cm long, origin at the middle of the shoulder line, +X forward, +Z up; `Art/Blender/README.md`) and drawn `BirdScale` (4) times that; the clearances use its own box without the asset's bounds extension for the flap. The placeholder is a sphere of the body's size (62 x 20 x 14 cm nominal) times `BirdScale`. It casts a shadow. It circles at the followed ant's height (at least `MinCircleHeight`) on the upwind side, its centre pushed out until the whole circle keeps `ClearMargin` plus half its span from the field. The dive turns in along a Bezier and comes in along a corridor checked clear with `Field.March` (between the bark normal and upwind first), flares to a stop with its belly to the bark `PassGap` out (`PassHeight`, raised to clear the tallest piece carried nearby by `PieceClearance`), then climbs off along the surface (up the trunk, or up and upwind off a flat top), never on through the wood. Wing beats are held back wherever the field says the wings have no room (a tip falls 30 cm x `BirdScale` below the shoulder line at `FlapAmount` 1).
- **Camera:** no part of the visible bird comes within `CameraClearance` (100 cm) of the camera (pushed off the bark, or sideways). While a dive targets the controlled ant, `AAntCamera` turns its look direction (never its position, so its wood clearance holds) toward a point `NudgeBlend` (0.5) of the way from the ant to the bird (`ABirdSystem::GetCameraNudge`), eased in over `NudgeIn` (0.6 s) from the call and out over `NudgeOut` (1 s) after the pass; the player's own look input fades it out for that dive; `NudgeKeepAnt` (1.1) keeps the ant within that fraction of half the vertical field of view (2 or more: no limit), and the boom eases out to `NudgeBoomScale` (1.8) times its length with the same weight; the camera's own wood handling (occlusion, ground, thick wood) still applies to the longer boom.
- **Sounds:** `bird_call` at the start of the dive and `bird_pass` at the pass, placed in the world at the bird (`LeafSound::PlayAt`, `ULeaflineAudioSubsystem::PlayOneShotAt`: attenuated and spatialised, full volume within 30 m, silent past 150 m). Both files are in `AudioRaw`; if one is missing it falls back to `gust_warning` or `shift_whoosh`, chosen by checking the file, so nothing logs a warning.
- **HUD:** one line in the wind panel, in the plain text colour: a gliding bird icon and "circling", or a stooping, blinking one and "diving!" (`Leaf.HudIcons 0`: "Bird circling" / "Bird diving!"); the panel grows by one row only while it shows. On the first bird day, and on the first bird day of each game session (display only, so a save past its first bird day still sees it once), the hint "A bird is out today. It swoops at ants carrying pieces on exposed bark: stay off the red, or grip when it dives." (with bait: "..., grip when it dives, or drop your piece in the open to draw it away.") in the goals' style, while the bird is about and no goal shows. During a dive at the player's ant: "Bird diving! Hold Space to grip" (and the drop key when allowed); gripping then: "Gripping. Hold on until the bird has passed".
- **Bait (tier 2, `Leaf.Bait`, on by default):** G / pad B (`IA_Drop`; icon `Key_G`) while the bird circles or dives and the ant carries a piece: the piece lies on the bark (a thin green disc), the ant is as before its cut (`AAntPawn::DropCarried` clears the piece and the cut record, so dropping makes no line). A piece on exposed bark beats every ant as a target, also mid-dive (a piece dropped in the open during a dive turns the bird to it); the bird takes it and stays away `BaitAwaySeconds`. One on sheltered bark lies there until the day ends. Bait swoops count toward the day's swoops.
- `FBirdTuning` (`Leaf.Tune bird`): `bBird` (on), `bBait` (on), `FirstBirdDay` 3 (recruits off), `FirstBirdDayRecruits` 2 (recruits on), `MaxSwoopsPerDay` 2, `FirstDaySwoops` 1, `SwoopInterval` 40 s, `SwoopJitter` 0.3, `LookAgain` 4 s, `WarnSeconds` 2.5, `CommitSeconds` 1.0 (a piece dropped in the last 1.0 s before the pass does not turn the bird; it stays as bait), `AwaySeconds` 30, `BaitAwaySeconds` 45, `HitRadius` 120, `MaxHitsPerSwoop` 2; look: `BirdScale` 4, `PassHeight` 150, `PieceClearance` 30, `CircleRadius` 800, `CircleDistance` 2500 (about 17 to 33 m from the player), `MinCircleHeight` 1500, `ClearMargin` 450, `GlideSpeed` 900, `ApproachDistance` 1800, `ExitDistance` 2400, `ExitSeconds` 1.6, `LeaveSeconds` 4, `FlapHz` 3.5; sound volumes.

`Tools/tree_test.sh auto20` (twenty AutoPlay days) cannot show the bird: AutoPlay ends every shift at once, and the bird only acts in shifts. With the bird off it reproduces bd-base's fillets-off baseline exactly (470.2 hauled, end card after day 16); with the bird on, identically (zero swoops). The bird's cost was measured with `auto20hold` (the third scout stays out 240 sim s with its piece, so the shift lasts), against its own bird-off control:

| scenario | bird off | bird on | end card off / on |
|---|---|---|---|
| hold240, scout never grips, 3 swoops | 466.2 | 340.6 | 17 / 19 |
| hold240 holdgrip, 3 swoops | 465.9 | 387.9 | 17 / 18 |
| hold240 holdgrip, 2 swoops | 465.9 | 396.7 | 17 / 17 |
| hold240 holdgrip, 1 swoop | 465.9 | 396.7 | 17 / 17 |
| after the review fixes, hold end scheduled, holdgrip, 2 swoops | 469.5 | 429.5 | 17 / 17 |

A scout that never grips loses the piece that would lay its line, which costs the whole line. Hence `MaxSwoopsPerDay` 2.

## Colony, days and saving

- `LeaflineGameInstance` holds the persistent colony: leaf mass to spend, total hauled, upgrade levels, day number, base seed. `LeaflineSaveGame` writes it to one slot.
- `DayDirector` runs the day as a state machine:

```
Title -> DayStart (one tree off: grow tree from hash(seed, day); on: keep the stage's tree, or grow it on a move
                   or after loading; set wind, reset roster and recruit pool) [-> Move card on a new tree]
      -> Shift (player controls scout i; others may be Returning; recruits work lines at 1x)
      -> Harvest (no scout waiting or out, recruits still have work: 5x, skippable)
      -> AllHome -> Recap [-> EndCard, once, after the third milestone] -> Nest (upgrade screen) -> DayStart
```

- `ADayDirector::UpdateDayFlow` runs every frame in `Shift` and `Harvest`: with no scout waiting or out (and the send-home or fall moment over), it enters the harvest if `ALineSystem::HasWork()`, else ends the day once no scout is still returning. In the harvest the day ends when no recruit has work and no scout is returning. `HasWork` is true while any recruit is queued or out, or one in the nest has trips left and a live line to go to.

- The roster holds one entry per worker: `Waiting`, `Out`, `Returning`, `Home`, `BlownOff`. "Next worker" spawns at the nest. When none are waiting, the camera follows a returning ant and the cycle key switches between them until all are in.
- Difficulty per day (one tree off): `LowestLeafyHeight` rises, gust peak and frequency rise. With one tree per stage, see below.
- Spawn objects the pawn depends on in `InitGame`, since the pawn exists before `BeginPlay`.
- Milestones and the end card read `TotalHauled`, against the line set or the "by hand" set (`ULeaflineGameInstance::AreRecruitsEnabled`). With one tree per stage the sets are 25 / 190 / 335 with recruits (since 15 Nov: recruits from day 1, Brood ladder 2 / 3 / 4 / 6 / 8 / 10 / 12, and the climb curve; 25 / 155 / 270 went with the linear curve) and 5 / 60 / 95 by hand (`Game/Upgrades.cpp`, `docs/economy.md`); the tree moves at the second and third milestones, the end card comes at the third.
- **One tree per colony stage** (`FDayTuning::bOneTree`, default on; `Leaf.OneTree`; spec `docs/briefs/one-tree.md`). The save holds the stage, its tree seed, the stage's first day, the tree's leaf count, the bitten leaves (`FSavedLeaf`) and the last day's routes (`FSavedTrail`, with their day). `StartDay` with the flag on: seed 0 means no tree yet (a fresh colony or an older save), so it picks the stage from the milestones and `SeedForStage(stage)`; the tree is grown only when its seed, stage or the game instance's tree generation (bumped by `SetTree` with a new stage or seed, `ResetSave` and every load) differ from the grown one, otherwise it is kept as it is, bites and all (a kept day start takes about 2 ms, a grown one about 400 ms). Shape comes from the stage (`Stage % 4`: the old giant, a spreading giant, a tall spire, a leaning elder), and the stage's `FStageTuning` sets trunk height, lowest leafy height, richness, gusts and the sparse low zone. Yesterday's routes are loaded when they are from the day before. `EndDay` puts the tree's bites and today's routes into the colony together with the day's haul, in one `SaveNow`; a purchase or the end card saves later and carries that snapshot. If the colony's milestones (under the one-tree sets) now call for a later stage, `EndDay` also moves it one stage (`SetTree`: new seed, from the next day, bites and routes cleared); at most one move a day. The next `StartDay` from the nest shows `EDayPhase::Move`, the card "The colony moves to …", with the camera on the whole new tree; at launch on such a day the card follows the title. Quitting mid-day loses that day's bites and haul alike: neither was written.
- Save version 3 adds the tree fields. Versions 1 and 2 never stored their version (it equalled the class default), so `Version` now defaults to 0 and a new colony sets 3 explicitly; a file that loads as 0 is migrated: days, upgrades and haul kept, tree seed 0 (a fresh tree for the stage its milestones give).
- Save version 2 adds `BestDayHaul`; version-1 saves load with their progress (levels clamped to the new range). Unreal's save serialisation skips properties equal to their defaults, so a version-1 file never stored its `Version` and then simply loaded as current (checked on a copy of the owner's day-7 save); since version 3 it loads as 0 and is migrated (above). `-LeafSlot=<name>` selects another save slot for automated runs (`Tools/line_test.sh` uses `LineTest`).

## World and lighting

- Sky atmosphere, directional sun, real-time sky light, exponential height fog with volumetric fog for light shafts, virtual shadow maps.
- The sun's elevation is set by the shift number.
- `FLookTuning` on the day director (`Leaf.Tune look`) holds sun, sky, fog and haze values. Look pass (13 Nov, `docs/reviews/look-shots.md`): sky light 3 with a cool tint (0.75, 0.85, 1.15) so shadowed bark gets a cool fill (Lumen GI still carries most of it), and the camera's exposure bias 0.2 (was -0.4). The dark sky in the first shift's look-up views is the low morning sun (22°) plus auto exposure metering on sunlit bark, not a designed storm; tuning cannot lift it without overexposing the bark. The amber wash on near bark was not haze but the cutaway digging a trench into the bark under a camera 12 cm above it; the fix was `WoodClearance` 60 (at least `CutawayRadius`); since look pass 2 the bark cutaway opens only when wood hides the ant, and `WoodClearance` is 30 (32% sky in the upper frame looking up from the trunk, 19% at 60). Fog values unchanged: no fog change measurably helped.
- Ground: a large procedural plane with a soil and leaf-litter shader, included in the field so the ant can walk on it; beyond the ants' reach the visible ground rolls into hills (`Leaf.Relief`, see Field). A soft boundary turns the ant back a short distance from the trunk.
- A ring of distant plain trunks fading into fog suggests a grove (`FTreeParams` `Grove*`, with an understory, `Leaf.GroveUnder`).
- The project has Substrate, Lumen, hardware ray tracing and SM6 enabled from the template. **Verify** early that a packaged build runs on a second machine or at least outside the editor; if hardware ray tracing causes trouble, turn it off rather than debug it.

## Audio

- Sources are synthesised by script (`docs/briefs/audio.md`) and loaded from WAV data at runtime (the editor MCP has no audio import).
- Wind loops whose volumes follow `Strength` (`FWindTuning`, from calm to a full gust: `wind_calm_loop` `CalmVolume` 0.19 to `CalmVolumeGust` 0.12, `leaves_rustle_loop` 0.07 to 0.47, `wind_gust_loop` up to `GustVolume` 0.97). Since 15 Nov the calm bed is about 4.5 dB under its old level (calm 0.32, rustle 0.12) and a full gust is as loud as before; the gust loop has no whistle. Footsteps (`step_01..06`) triggered from the gait phase; the cut loop and its finish; pickup; deposit chime; send home; shift whoosh; grip; blown off; stamina low; gust warning; milestone, upgrade and day-end jingles; the bird's call and pass; an ambient pad; UI ticks (`ui_move`, `ui_confirm`, `ui_deny`).
- Plain audio components and attenuation are enough. No middleware.

### Runtime WAV playback (written by Codex)

`Art/Audio/*.wav` are copied to `Content/Leafline/AudioRaw/` with the extension `.wavraw`, so the editor never auto-imports them, and staged into the pak with `+DirectoriesToAlwaysStageAsUFS=(Path="Leafline/AudioRaw")` in `DefaultGame.ini`. `ULeaflineAudioSubsystem` (a world subsystem) loads and caches 48 kHz 16-bit PCM, mono or stereo, and plays 2D sounds through `USoundWaveProcedural`: `PlayOneShot(Name, Volume, Pitch)` and `StartLoop(Name, Volume)` returning a `ULeaflineAudioLoop` with `SetVolume`, `SetPitch` and `Stop`. Loops refill on underflow without a gap. During the harvest's fast-forward (`Lines->GetSpeedFactor()` above 1, pushed each frame by `ADayDirector::Tick` through `SetPlaybackSpeed`) one-shots are ducked to volume × speed^-0.7 (about a third at x5) and a sound, with its `_NN` variants counted together (`step_01..06`), starts at most twice per 0.25 s; each start is logged as `Audio: fast-forward start`, and the end logs how many starts were dropped. `ui_*`, `milestone`, `upgrade` and `day_end` are exempt, and the speed is back to 1 when the day ends, so the `day_end` jingle plays at full volume 1.5 s after the fast-forward, not over it. Loops are not touched. Test with `Leaf.Sound deposit 1 1.2`. After regenerating the audio, copy the files again. Background music: `Tools/audio/make_music.py <outdir>` renders the options (48 kHz 16-bit stereo loops, about -20 LUFS); `AWindSystem::UpdateMusic` plays `FWindTuning::MusicSound` (`music_loop` by default) from `AudioRaw` as a loop at `MusicVolume` (0.25) while `bMusic` is on (off by default: no option is picked yet; picking one means copying that render to `AudioRaw/<MusicSound>.wavraw` and turning `bMusic` on), and the pad drops from `PadVolume` (0.22) to `PadVolumeWithMusic` (0), the calm wind's and the rustle's baselines to `CalmVolumeWithMusic` (0.125) and `RustleVolumeWithMusic` (0.047), about 3.5 dB under 0.19 / 0.07 (gusts still swell as without music). It follows the master volume like every loop, switches while playing (`Leaf.Tune wind bMusic True`, `Leaf.Tune wind MusicSound <file>`) and logs `Music: <file> at <volume>, pad <volume> (loaded 0|1)`; a missing file is tried once per name.

**Button prompt icons** work the same way: 27 PNGs from Xelu's CC0 prompt pack (`docs/sourced-assets.md`), cropped to each key's shape, 64 px tall, in `Content/Leafline/UIRaw/*.pngraw`, staged with `+DirectoriesToAlwaysStageAsUFS=(Path="Leafline/UIRaw")`. `LeafPrompts::Get` (`UI/PromptIcons.*`) loads them with `FImageUtils::ImportBufferAsTexture2D` and caches them. HUD text can hold tokens (`{Cut}`, `{Grip}`, `{Send}`, `{Follow}`, `{Cycle}`, `{Sprint}`, `{Pause}`, `{Drop}`, `{Confirm}`, `{Back}`, `{Move}`, `{Look}`, `{Choose}`, `{Change}`, and `{orclick}`, which reads " or click" on keyboard and mouse only): `DrawTextCentered` / `DrawTextAt` draw them as icons for the device used last (`ALeaflinePlayerController::IsUsingGamepad`, from `InputKey`), light keyboard keys or Xbox Series buttons for every pad (owner's choice; no PlayStation glyphs). A missing icon falls back to the key's name.

## Performance budget

- Field: a few thousand curve segments. Each ant needs about 20 evaluations per frame, the camera about 30 per frame. With per-branch bounds this is well under a millisecond for ten ants.
- Tree mesh: up to about 1–2 million triangles with bark relief, built once per day. A build under two seconds is acceptable behind the day-start screen. Generate on a worker thread if it hitches.
- Leaves: 20–40 thousand quads, masked. Watch overdraw.
- Target 60 fps at 1080p on a mid-range GPU.
- Measured 2026-11-12 (`Tools/perf_packaged.sh`, packaged Development build, 1920x1080 windowed, the owner's NVIDIA GPU with ray tracing tier 1.1). Every run sat at the display's 60 Hz, so the GPU time is the real headroom:

  | Run | fps (avg / 1% low) | Game thread | GPU |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Trunk, D3D12 SM6, ray tracing | 60 / 53 | 1.6 ms | 4.2 ms |
  | Canopy (twigs and leaves), ray tracing | 60 / 52 | 1.7 ms | 4.0 ms |
  | Trunk, `-noraytracing` | 60 / 53 | 1.6 ms | 4.2 ms |
  | Canopy, `-noraytracing` | 60 / 55 | 1.5 ms | 4.2 ms |
  | Trunk, `-dx11` (SM5) | 60 / 54 | 1.4 ms | not comparable |

  After look pass 1 (8645 leaves instead of 5691, 12600 grove crown leaves, stronger fog scattering), same runs: trunk 4.2 ms, canopy 4.6 ms GPU with ray tracing; 4.4 and 4.4 ms without. Still 60 fps, 1% lows 50 to 53. With the leaf material masked for its cutaway: trunk 4.6, canopy 5.0 ms with ray tracing; 4.3 and 4.9 ms without. After the look pass (13 Nov: `M_TreeBark2`, boom 170, the new sky fill), Epic: trunk 4.2, canopy 4.1 ms with ray tracing; 4.3 and 4.0 ms without; Low (`scalability 0`): 1.5 and 1.3 ms with, 1.6 and 1.2 ms without. Still 60 fps, 1% lows 52 to 54. The new bark costs nothing measurable (the closer camera changes the views slightly). `EXTRA_CMDS` in `perf_packaged.sh` adds commands to every run.

  Without hardware ray tracing the game starts and runs the same ("Ray tracing is disabled. Reason: not supported"); the GPU cost does not change, so nothing depends on it. DX11 also starts and runs. Reference shots (`Tools/ref_shots.sh`, last taken after look pass 1 and the material changes) with and without ray tracing look nearly the same (without it Lumen falls back to software tracing, and the procedural tree has no mesh distance field), so the look does not depend on it. A mid-range GPU is perhaps three to five times slower than this one, so 4.2 ms leaves room for 60 fps there, unchecked.

## Risks, in order

1. **Camera feel.** Only the owner can judge it. Get it into their hands on day one.
2. **Lighting on the procedural mesh.** Check with a screenshot in the first hour of tree work.
3. **Field and mesh disagreeing** at junctions or after bark relief. `Leaf.DebugField` exists for this.
4. **Packaging.** Do a packaged build as soon as the loop runs; do not leave it for Sunday.
5. **Disk space.** Drive D: had 57 GB free on 2026-11-12. Cooked builds and the derived data cache are large.
