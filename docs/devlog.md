## How this game is made

Leafline is made by one person directing AI agents. Claude (Anthropic) does planning and most of the programming, driving the Unreal editor through Epic's MCP plugin. Codex (OpenAI) makes concept art and reviews code. The ant model is generated by a Blender script, and the audio is synthesised by a script. The tree, bark, leaves and effects are procedural: no textures and no hand-made tree models.

**Earlier prototype.** On 1–2 November I made a quick prototype of a tree-climbing ant game in a separate project (`TreeAntGame`). No files from it are used here: no code, assets, materials or models. This project was created on 12 November and all game content in it was made from that day on. What did carry over is knowledge: my idea notes (`concept-main.md`), and workflow and engine lessons from that prototype's notes, which the planning agent read (its `CLAUDE.md` and to-do list, not its source code) and used when writing the plan.

**Pre-jam tooling.** Two helper scripts come from another of my projects and predate the jam, as the rules allow for tooling: `Scripts/unreal-lock.sh` (keeps one Unreal process running on the machine at a time) and `Scripts/RunGame.bat` (starts the game standalone). They were adapted to this project; they contain no game content.

## 2026-11-12 (Thu): planning

- Created the project from the Third Person template at 10:05 CET. The template content will be deleted.
- Codex produced five concept images from the idea notes (`Art/Concept/Codex/2026-11-12/initial`). Took four reference photos looking up a real trunk for scale.
- Spent the morning in a planning interview with Claude (Fable 5.2). It read both jams' rules, including the MegaJam Official Rules, and asked me 25 questions over four rounds.
- I had not realised the MegaJam had a theme. "Director's Cut" turned the ant into a **leafcutter**: you choose how big a piece to cut, and your scent trail directs the workers who follow it.
- Game Off's theme, SHIFT, is covered by the same mechanic: sending a loaded ant home shifts you to the next worker. The returning ant simply replays its recorded trail backwards, which made this cheap enough to attempt by Sunday.
- Rocket launchers against birds, from my original notes, are gone. The MegaJam rules exclude anything that reads as animal cruelty, and distraction fits the game better anyway.
- Technical bet: the tree is a distance field. Walking, the camera, wind shelter and trails all query that one function instead of physics collision.
- Wrote `design.md`, `tech.md`, `plan.md`, agent briefs and `CLAUDE.md`. Building moves to a cheaper model from here.

## 2026-11-12 (Thu): bootstrap and the first walk

- Deleted the Third Person template content and added the C++ module. The first build compiled on the first try.
- The tree grows from a seed in about 150 ms: a trunk with a root flare and roots, 22 limbs, about 120 boughs and 850 twigs, roughly 830,000 triangles of smooth tubes. Growth bends branches away from each other so they don't pass through one another.
- The ant walks on the distance field, not on collision. In an unattended run it walked from the ground up the trunk, across limbs, boughs and twigs, with no stalls and a projection error that never went above 0.07 cm.
- Lighting check passed: the procedural mesh casts and receives shadows under Lumen.
- Watching that unattended run made me motion sick. The camera's up followed the ant's up, so walking round a thin twig rolled the whole view. Now the horizon stays level on thin branches, follows the surface only on big limbs and the trunk, and can't roll faster than 45° per second. `Leaf.Tune` lets me change camera values while playing.
- In parallel, a Claude agent wrote the Blender script for the ant mesh, another synthesised the sound effects, and Codex painted key art and reference studies.

## 2026-11-12 (Thu): the loop and the wind

- M2 landed in one pass: leaves on the twigs, cutting with a growing size arc, the fragment carried overhead, scent trails recorded at half an ant length and drawn as ribbons, send home with the worker walking back on its own along its trail while control shifts to the next one, three workers a day, deposits at the nest, a recap titled "today's cut", and a new tree the next day.
- First packaged build: the cook failed on a missing asset-manager rule (pulled in by the editor toolset plugins); with that fixed, the packaged game starts, grows the tree and runs a day.
- Codex reviewed the M1 code and found six real problems (controls reversing when the camera looks up from below, a camera trap behind branches, sphere tracing that could overshoot or give up early, failed projections being kept, the frame-time clamp). All fixed; its review is in `docs/reviews/codex-m1.md`.
- Codex also wrote the save game and the five upgrades on a separate branch in its own worktree; I reviewed and merged it.
- M3: one prevailing wind per day with a gust cycle. Exposure comes from which way the surface faces and whether the tree shelters it upwind. In a full gust, a large fragment on the windward side of the trunk is blown off (force 1.16 against a grip of 1.00); the same fragment on the lee side rides it out. A blown-off ant falls, lands and walks home empty-handed. The cutting arc is coloured by the predicted risk of each size.
- Fixed the ant floating at the base of the trunk: the distance field blended the trunk into the ground with an 80 cm fillet the mesh doesn't have. The blends are now small enough not to show.
