## How this game is made

Leafline is made by one person directing AI agents. Claude (Anthropic) does planning and most of the programming, driving the Unreal editor through Epic's MCP plugin. Codex (OpenAI) makes concept art and reviews code. The ant model is generated by a Blender script, and the audio is synthesised by a script. The tree, bark, leaves and effects are procedural: no textures and no hand-made tree models.

**Earlier prototype.** On 1–2 November I made a quick prototype of a tree-climbing ant game in a separate project (`TreeAntGame`). No files from it are used here: no code, assets, materials or models. This project was created on 12 November and all game content in it was made from that day on. What did carry over is knowledge: my idea notes (`concept-main.md`), and workflow and engine lessons from that prototype's notes, which the planning agent read (its `CLAUDE.md` and to-do list, not its source code) and used when writing the plan.

**Pre-jam tooling.** Two helper scripts come from another of my projects and predate the jam, as the rules allow for tooling: `Scripts/unreal-lock.sh` (limits how many Unreal processes run on the machine at once) and `Scripts/RunGame.bat` (starts the game standalone). They were adapted to this project; they contain no game content. The level `L_Main` started as a copy of the engine's own default template map (sky, sun, sky light, fog, clouds); its floor and props were removed.

## 2026-11-12 (Thu): planning

- Created the project from the Third Person template at 10:05 CET. The template content will be deleted.
- Codex produced five concept images from the idea notes (`Art/Concept/Codex/2026-11-12/initial`). Took four reference photos looking up a real trunk for scale.

- I had not realised the MegaJam had a theme. "Director's Cut" turned the ant into a **leafcutter**: you choose how big a piece to cut, and your scent trail directs the workers who follow it.
- Game Off's theme, SHIFT, is covered by the same mechanic: sending a loaded ant home shifts you to the next worker. The returning ant simply replays its recorded trail backwards, which made this cheap enough to attempt by Sunday.
- Rocket launchers against birds, from my original notes, are gone. The MegaJam rules exclude anything that reads as animal cruelty, and distraction fits the game better anyway.
- Technical bet: the tree is a distance field. Walking, the camera, wind shelter and trails all query that one function instead of physics collision.
- Wrote `design.md`, `tech.md`, `plan.md`, agent briefs and `CLAUDE.md`. Building moves to a cheaper model from here.

## 2026-11-12 (Thu): bootstrap and the first walk

- Deleted the Third Person template content and added the C++ module. The first build compiled on the first try.
- The tree grows from a seed in about 150 ms: a trunk with a root flare and roots, 22 limbs, about 120 boughs and 850 twigs, roughly 830,000 triangles of smooth tubes. Growth bends branches away from each other so they don't pass through one another.
- The ant walks on the distance field, not on collision. In an unattended run it walked from the ground up the trunk, across limbs, boughs and twigs, with no stalls and a projection error that never went above 0.07 cm.
- Lighting check passed: the procedural mesh casts and receives shadows under Lumen.
- Watching that unattended run made me motion sick. The camera's up followed the ant's up, so walking round a thin twig rolled the whole view. Now the horizon stays level on thin branches, follows the surface only on big limbs and the trunk, and can't roll faster than 45° per second. `Leaf.Tune` lets me change camera values while playing.
- In parallel, a Claude agent wrote the Blender script for the ant mesh, another synthesised the sound effects, and Codex painted key art and reference studies.

## 2026-11-12 (Thu): the loop and the wind

- M2 landed in one pass: leaves on the twigs, cutting with a growing size arc, the fragment carried overhead, scent trails recorded at half an ant length and drawn as ribbons, send home with the worker walking back on its own along its trail while control shifts to the next one, three workers a day, deposits at the nest, a recap titled "today's cut", and a new tree the next day.
- First packaged build: the cook failed on a missing asset-manager rule (pulled in by the editor toolset plugins); with that fixed, the packaged game starts, grows the tree and runs a day.
- Codex reviewed the M1 code and found six real problems (controls reversing when the camera looks up from below, a camera trap behind branches, sphere tracing that could overshoot or give up early, failed projections being kept, the frame-time clamp). All fixed; its review is in `docs/reviews/codex-m1.md`.
- Codex also wrote the save game and the five upgrades on a separate branch in its own worktree; I reviewed and merged it.
- M3: one prevailing wind per day with a gust cycle. Exposure comes from which way the surface faces and whether the tree shelters it upwind. In a full gust, a large fragment on the windward side of the trunk is blown off (force 1.16 against a grip of 1.00); the same fragment on the lee side rides it out. A blown-off ant falls, lands and walks home empty-handed. The cutting arc is coloured by the predicted risk of each size.
- Fixed the ant floating at the base of the trunk: the distance field blended the trunk into the ground with an 80 cm fillet the mesh doesn't have. The blends are now small enough not to show.

## 2026-11-12 (Thu): first playtest

- The owner's first playtest gave seven notes; all are fixed.
- The ant still climbed through the air at the base of the trunk. The real cause was not the ground blend after all: the walking surface rounded every joint of a branch with a sphere of the joint radius, and where the trunk's buried section meets the flare that sphere was 14 m across and poked up to 1.7 m out of the bark. The field now uses the exact cone between samples, with the joint rounded only on the outer side of a bend, where the mesh rounds it too. Checked against every visible vertex of the bark (436,644): none is more than 0.00 cm off the walking surface. Walking tests on trunk, limb, bough and twig: no stalls, projection error at most 0.06 cm.
- Ants now start on top of the nest entrance; the mound is part of the walking surface.
- HUD text is rendered at its real size, so it is sharp. The cut label now says what it is about: "This piece: safe / risky / too big for a gust".
- New bark material without UV seams: the noise is evaluated on a cylinder around each branch, stretched along it, so the fissures run up the trunk and out along the limbs.
- The camera no longer jumps in when wood comes between it and the ant. It passes through, and the bark along the line to the ant dithers away.
- A Codex review of the screens, audio and pause code found five more issues (sounds playing through pause, an end card that could be lost, a stuck state at twig tips, among others). All fixed.
- Two lessons for the tools, now in `CLAUDE.md`: Live Coding scrambled the debug console commands after new ones were added, and deleting material nodes from a script crashed the editor.

## 2026-11-12 (Thu night): the line

- New core feature from the owner's brief (`docs/briefs/the-line.md`): when a scout's piece is on its way home, its route becomes a *line*, and recruits from the colony walk it, cut the same size of piece from the leaves around the scout's cut and carry them home while you scout on. Patches run dry, so lines have to keep coming.
- Recruits are the same ants as the scouts, driven by a new line system on a fixed 1/120 s clock together with the wind's gust clock, so the end-of-day harvest (5×, skippable) runs the same simulation however fast it is shown.
- Leaves now have a value: pale young leaves high and far out are worth up to three times as much. The patch around the leaf you are looking at is outlined with what it is worth, and trails glow by how much their patch has left.
- Upgrades have six levels; Brood is now the recruit pool. Saves move to version 2 without losing the old colony, and remember the best day.
- Work split: the main session wrote the line core; a Claude agent did leaf richness, patch membership and walkable approach routes over the branch graph; Codex did the ladders, save migration and an economy model (end card at about 17 minutes with the line, 18.5 without); a second Claude agent did the "+N" pops, colony counter, line rows and recap. Codex then reviewed the day-end state machine and found seven problems, all fixed.
- Found on the way: an ant following a route got pinned where the ground meets the trunk, because the point it followed ran ahead and the straight step toward it cut into the wood. The followed point now waits for the ant. This helps returning scouts too.

## 2026-11-13 (Fri): one tree

- New brief from the owner (`docs/briefs/one-tree.md`), from a playtest note: the colony no longer gets a new tree every day. It lives on one tree; the leaves it cuts stay cut, across quitting too, and never grow back. The low boughs are sparse and run out in a few days, so the colony has to climb into more wind. At the second and third milestones it moves to a new, taller tree with richer leaves and stronger gusts, and a card says so. Yesterday's trails stay for one more day, faint. Off switch: `Leaf.OneTree 0`, which plays exactly as before (checked leaf by leaf against the old trees).
- The save now holds the tree: its stage and seed, and every bitten leaf. The day's bites go into the same write as the day's haul, so quitting mid-day restarts the day with neither. A relaunch regrows the tree from its seed and applies the bites before the leaves are meshed once. Older saves never stored their version number (Unreal skips values equal to the default), so version 3 defaults to 0 and treats 0 as old.
- Leaves that can no longer be cut look bare from a distance: the blade goes and the stalk stays.
- Work split: the main session wrote the day flow, save and wind; a Claude agent did the leaf density, bare stalks, saved bites and a tree report; two smaller Claude agents did yesterday's trails and the HUD (move card, hints, meter marks); Codex rebuilt the economy model on the measured trees (end card at 17 minutes with recruits, 18.6 by hand, four days on the first tree) and reviewed the save, load and day start: nine findings over two passes, all fixed.
- Testing needed a scripted colony that behaves a little like a player: it now buys recruits first and cuts the lowest leaves left, bough by bough. In twenty such days the mean height of the day's cuts on the first tree climbed from 37 m to 60 m before the move, and the three lowest boughs were bare by the fourth morning.

## 2026-11-13 (Fri afternoon): playtest notes

- Wind map: hold Tab (LB on a pad) and the bark round the ant fills with coloured dots, green sheltered to red exposed, from the same exposure test as the HUD's "Here: ..." line. A tap of Tab still switches workers. A Claude agent wrote the overlay from a brief; the main session did the input, HUD and material.

- When the last scout heads home with no recruit trips (day 1, for one), its walk home now plays fast and can be skipped.
- Scent trails got a first smoke look: a soft drifting haze with an upright ribbon, fading with distance.

## 2026-11-13 (Fri evening): look pass

- A new brief from an art review of three of the owner's screenshots (`docs/briefs/look-pass.md`): the black wedges under the ant, bark that read as drawn-on outlines, a hazy amber wash on near bark, small HUD text.
- The black wedge was bark buried inside the neighbouring branch at a junction, seen through the camera cutaway and lit by nothing. The tree mesh now writes, per vertex, how deep it lies inside another branch, and the new bark material hides what is buried. The amber wash was not haze at all: the camera sat 12 cm above the trunk and the cutaway dug a trench under it, so the player looked past the trunk. It now keeps 60 cm from thick wood.
- New bark (`M_TreeBark2`, the old one stays behind `Leaf.Bark 0`): long ridges and wide soft furrows, each ridge its own tone, relief in the lighting only, fine grain up close, moss in patches. The first try was Voronoi plates, which looked like paving; stretching them twelve times along the branch made ridges. A layer of small cracks drew looping outlines again and was dropped. The shader code lives in `Tools/materials/` and goes into the asset over MCP.
- Cooler, stronger sky fill for shadowed bark; exposure up a little; the camera a little closer (170 cm) and a little higher. The dark morning sky in the owner's shots is the low first-shift sun and the exposure metering on sunlit bark, not a bug.
- The HUD has one style table: panels behind each group, text at least 1.85% of the screen height, worker states by name, a labelled stamina bar.
- Work split: a Claude agent took the reference shots, tuned the lighting and diagnosed the wedge (`docs/reviews/look-shots.md`); Codex restyled the HUD in a worktree; a smaller Claude agent wrote the buried-bark pass in the mesh builder; the main session wrote the bark shader, the camera changes and the material over MCP.
- The owner's first look at the new build found holes where roots and big limbs meet the trunk: the buried-bark mask cut real surface. Three rounds to fix: an 8-bit distance clamped too short, children whose open base pokes out of the trunk, and finally interpolation across 300 cm trunk triangles, which on the owner's own tree cut bark up to 2 m "inside" the trunk (a Claude agent loaded the owner's save in a test slot, compared old and new bark at the same spot and swept the depth). The depth now scales with the size of the branch.
- Branch collars (the owner's polish note): limbs, boughs and roots swell where they leave their parent, in the walking surface and the mesh alike. The bark pattern follows a radius without the flare and collars, so the trunk base shows long ridges instead of paving stones.
- A faint line in the corner (the owner's idea) shows the build, the tree and a pasteable `Leaf.Goto` for the view on screen, so a screenshot is enough to reproduce a bug.

## 2026-11-14 (Sat): the bird, the floor and a night run

- Late Friday, look pass 2: the bark cutaway opens only when wood really hides the ant and keeps a faint dithered layer, the nest mound is generated in Blender with its own soil, the camera can look further up, and fillets smooth the joints where branches meet, in the walking surface and on the mesh. The ant's head got a neck.
- The bird, the stretch goal from the first plan: a small bird circles on the wind and swoops at ants carrying pieces on exposed bark. It never touches them; the gust from its wings knocks them off, and gripping or shelter keeps them on. Dropping your piece in the open draws it away. Its mesh comes from a Blender script and its call and wing rush from the audio script. It stayed switchable for the owner's call.
- Wind you can see: a wall of dust and pollen blows in a couple of seconds ahead of each gust, leaves and grass sway harder, and the ant's own spot shows how exposed it is.
- The forest floor: grass from the clearing edge, leaf litter and twigs round the roots, and, seen from high on the tree, a leaf-flecked ground fading into haze. The ant got a copper and mahogany look.
- Ants walk on the fillets again, after a fix for recruits that got stuck at junctions, and twigs have fillets too.
- The interface: every line of text rewritten in plain words (`docs/ui-text.md`); icons for the wind, the bird, the load, the colony counter and the workers; a Controls page and a credits screen in the pause menu; and one colour per trail, fading with distance from your ant.
- For the jam page: a camera kit for the video (scripted orbit, crane, dolly and reveal shots, slow motion), new key art from Codex, and drafts of both itch pages and the submission form.
- The owner moved the feature freeze to Sunday. A night run added the veins and tone of its leaf to the carried piece, long bark ridges on the roots, an understory of smaller trees in front of the distant grove, rolling hills beyond the ants' reach, bark that blends across the fillet seams, and an option that makes higher leaves worth more (with `Leaf.Milestones` to try milestone sets for it).
